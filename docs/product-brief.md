# Earthform Product Brief

Load this document for product strategy, audience, positioning, content, brand, trust, or new experience decisions. Routine implementation that already has an approved contract does not need it.

## Thesis

Earthform turns complex geospatial and environmental capability into clear, adopted, commercially strong digital products. It is the product layer between technical capability and human decision.

Geospatial and environmental organizations increasingly work with satellite imagery, land registries, environmental indicators, operational records, public datasets, and territorial layers. Their recurring challenge is making this capability understandable, trustworthy, actionable, and easy to buy.

Earthform combines product strategy, research, information architecture, interaction design, interface quality, and commercial narrative with practical geospatial, environmental, land, and operational knowledge. It defines the decision a product must support, who makes it, which evidence matters, how uncertainty appears, and what experience makes technical capability legible and valuable.

## Audience

- Leaders of geoprocessing, territorial-intelligence, environmental-data, restoration, carbon, remote-sensing, land, and govtech companies.
- Product, operations, commercial, and technical leaders responsible for complex territorial software.
- Consultancies and technical organizations turning recurring expertise into scalable digital offers.

## Problems And Outcomes

Decision-makers often cannot identify what needs attention; operators depend on specialists for routine interpretation; strong engineering appears as layers, filters, tables, and jargon; commercial teams struggle to demonstrate value; and roadmaps fragment around isolated requests.

Earthform engagements should:

- turn territorial data into orientation for action;
- preserve rigor while reducing cognitive load;
- increase confidence and reduce avoidable specialist dependency;
- make value easier to demonstrate; and
- connect engineering, operations, product, and business through a coherent roadmap.

## Offer And Advantage

The current offer includes diagnostics, technical-platform repositioning, discovery, product definition, information architecture, flows, interface design, prototypes, and prioritization. Client work may reveal opportunities to co-create or incubate products, but that is an expansion path rather than the primary promise.

Earthform's advantage is the combination of product design, systems and interaction design, strategy and commercial narrative, and practical territorial knowledge. The differentiator is simplification without trivialization.

## Product And Experience Principles

1. Start with the decision, not the map.
2. Treat maps as evidence when spatial context improves understanding, not as decorative proof of sophistication.
3. Preserve rigor while progressively disclosing complexity.
4. Make source, coverage, recency, confidence, assumptions, and uncertainty inspectable.
5. Separate signal from noise: what happened, why it matters, what can be done, and how to monitor it.
6. Design for field, office, executive, technical, and public operating contexts.
7. Keep primary decisions visible; reveal methodology and advanced analysis when needed.
8. Treat research, information architecture, interaction, visual QA, and feedback as product infrastructure.
9. Never imply precision, causation, compliance, or impact unsupported by evidence.
10. Validate the decision model, workflow, and commercial value before scaling software.

## Design-Taste Baseline

- Public surfaces are quiet, grounded, trust-first, and selectively expressive; operational products require a separate direction.
- The approved public design system lives in `design-system/` and Figma file `v6MJtDY9WJeiaBrw7lLrZq`.
- Public landing coordinates: design variance `6/10`, motion `2/10`, density `6/10`.
- Avoid generic map dashboards, decorative geospatial layers, sci-fi environmental aesthetics, fake precision, excessive cards, and simplicity that hides methodological limits.
- Brand direction, map language, density model, visualization grammar, or a future operational system requires human approval and a DDR.

## Voice

Use clear Brazilian Portuguese by default. Be direct, precise, calm, contemporary, and evidence-led. Define necessary technical terms. Do not sound mystical, alarmist, extractive, or technologically grandiose. Distinguish facts, assumptions, estimates, and recommendations.

## Constraints

- Never invent client results, environmental outcomes, compliance claims, coverage, accuracy, or commercial proof.
- Never expose client data, sensitive locations, landholder information, credentials, private methodologies, or private artifacts without explicit approval and a documented security approach.
- Do not present legal, regulatory, environmental, carbon, or risk determinations as certain when professional review or incomplete data applies.
- Design for long geographic labels, multiple scales, missing or stale data, slow analysis, partial results, exports, and permission boundaries.
- Accessibility targets WCAG 2.2 AA and alternatives to purely spatial or color-dependent information.

## Open Decisions

Initial buyer and market focus, geography and jurisdiction, first design partner and use case, measurable outcomes, data classification and retention, mapping and visualization providers, hosting, analytics, CRM, contact operations, and deployment strategy remain uncommitted until explicitly approved.
